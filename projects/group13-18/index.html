How the Measurement Works

1. Observe the Sun: The Sun's altitude is the angle between the horizon and the Sun.

2. Align the arm: The index arm is moved so that it points toward the observed position of the Sun. Its reading represents the Sun's measured altitude.

3. Find the hour angle: The measured altitude is combined with the observer's latitude and the Sun's declination to determine the Sun's hour angle.

4. Relate it to solar noon: The hour angle tells us how far the Sun is from its position at local solar noon.

5. Convert angle to time: The Sun appears to move about 15° across the sky per hour because of Earth's rotation. Therefore, every 15° of hour angle corresponds to about 60 minutes of solar time.

About the Instrument

The Phalaka-Yantra was an astronomical instrument described by Bhāskara II.

The instrument consists of a rectangular board, a central pin, a graduated circle and a movable index arm.

Traditional dimensions: The board is described as 180 × 90 aṅgulas, with a 30-aṅgula radius graduated circle and a 60-aṅgula index arm.

90 horizontal divisions: The board is divided by 90 equally spaced horizontal lines, representing the traditional divisions described for the instrument.

Pivot position: The central pin is positioned at the middle of the 30th horizontal division, as described for the traditional instrument.

Historical reconstruction: This simulation is a reconstruction based on historical descriptions; no original Phalaka-Yantra specimen is known to survive.
