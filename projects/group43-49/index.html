01 · Project focus

Chapa Yantra and the clinometer concept

The Chapa Yantra is an arc-based traditional Indian measuring instrument associated with mathematical and observational practice. In this project, the focus is specifically on its connection with the clinometer principle: measuring an inclination angle and converting that angle into a useful height or distance measurement.

∠

Graduated angular scale

An arc divided into angular markings provides a numerical reading instead of only a visual estimate.

↓

Gravity reference

A plumb line, weighted pointer or gravity-stabilized mechanism provides a dependable vertical reference.

◉

Line of sight

The user sights the top or bottom of the target object while keeping the instrument correctly positioned.

△

Trigonometric calculation

The measured angle is combined with known dimensions to calculate an unknown height, distance or vertical difference.

02 · Working principle

From angle measurement to calculation

A clinometer-based measurement follows four clear stages. The instrument measures an angle; trigonometry then converts that angle into the physical quantity required by the experiment.

01

Measure the baseline

Measure the horizontal distance from the observer to the base of the target.

02

Sight the target

Aim the clinometer toward the required point, such as the top of a building, pole or tree.

03

Read the angle

Read the angle of elevation, depression or slope from the instrument's angular scale.

04

Calculate x

Use the appropriate tangent relationship with the known measurements to calculate the unknown quantity.

03 · Types of measurement

What can a clinometer measure?

The same instrument can be used in different configurations depending on whether the target is above, below or along an inclined surface.

↗

Angle of elevation

Used when the target lies above the observer's horizontal eye level.

↘

Angle of depression

Used when the target lies below the observer's horizontal eye level.

⌁

Slope or inclination

Used to measure the angle of an inclined surface such as a ramp, road, embankment or roof.

07 · Practical experiment

How to demonstrate it physically

A simple field experiment connects the Chapa Yantra idea, the clinometer and basic trigonometry in one activity.

01

Select a target

Choose a visible building, tree, pole or similar vertical object.

02

Measure distance

Measure the horizontal distance from your position to the object's base.

03

Measure eye height

Measure the vertical height of the clinometer or your eye level above the ground.

04

Take the angle

Sight the top of the object and record the elevation angle.

05

Apply tangent

Calculate the height above eye level using d × tan(θ), then add eye height.

06

Verify

Compare the calculated result with a known height or another measuring method if available.
