An easy guide to understanding how mechanical bows work (Anti-Gravity Mode)

📜 What is a Dhanur Yantra?

A Dhanur Yantra is an ancient mechanical bow (similar to a crossbow). Instead of holding the string with your hands, a latch holds it. This allows archers to pull back much heavier bows and release them smoothly with a trigger!

⚛️ The Main Physics Formula

STORED ENERGY FORMULA
Energy = ½ × Stiffness × (Pull Distance)²

🏹 Types of Bows: Potential Energy & Projectile Motion

Different bow materials store and transfer mechanical energy in distinct ways:

1. Bamboo Bow

Flexible / Easy Draw

Energy: Lowest Potential Energy.
Projectile Motion: Smooth, arc-like trajectory with lower arrow speeds.

2. Wood & Horn Bow (Composite)

Balanced Energy

Energy: Moderate Potential Energy.
Projectile Motion: Higher arrow speeds with a balanced arc trajectory.

3. Steel Bow (Crossbow / Dhanur Yantra)

Highest Stored Energy

Energy: Extremely high Potential Energy stored in rigid steel limbs.
Projectile Motion: Highest initial launch speed and flattest trajectory.

Quick Rules

• Stiffer materials store much more energy.

• Lighter arrows fly much faster than heavy ones.

• Anti-Gravity Field: Arrows accelerate upward as they fly!

💡 What We Have Learned From This Assessment

  • 1. Energy Storage: When we pull the bow back, muscle work turns into Potential Energy stored in the bent bow arms.
  • 2. Energy Release: The moment we pull the trigger, that trapped energy turns into Kinetic Energy (speed) to shoot the arrow.
  • 3. The Pull Effect: Pulling the bow twice as far back actually gives 4 times the stored energy, making the arrow travel much faster and farther.
  • 4. Ancient Engineering: Ancient engineers built mechanical bows so archers could shoot heavier, stronger arrows without getting tired.
